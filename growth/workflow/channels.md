## Problem-discovery sources

Research sources and acquisition channels have different purposes. A community may provide useful public accounts without permitting outreach; a review site may reveal friction without being a suitable place to recruit. Use [problem discovery](problem-discovery.md) for bounded collection and evidence evaluation.

Consider source categories according to the current question:

| Source category | Useful evidence | Limits to check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public communities and specialist forums | Tasks, attempted approaches, unresolved questions, and later outcomes | Audience relevance, first-hand versus second-hand accounts, dates, selection bias, access rules |
| Product marketplaces and review sites | Experiences after trying an existing solution, workarounds, reasons for switching, positive counterexamples | Product/version, region, duplicates, developer responses, possible resolution since posting |
| Comments on relevant educational or demonstration content | Questions left after an explanation, misunderstood terms, missing steps | Short comments may omit the task, attempted method, or final outcome |
| Local or professional groups | Context-specific constraints, suppliers, workflows, and collaboration problems | Membership, privacy, permitted research use, and separate contact authorization |
| First-party support, feedback, and authorized task observations | Actual product paths, reproducible failures, observed value, and successful alternatives | Existing storage/consent rules, release context, missing outcomes, and sampling bias |

Register only sources selected for a concrete investigation. Specific platforms, communities, audiences, search terms, and access findings belong to the configured project, not the shared template.

## Choosing actions

- For search channels, consider demand signals, page quality, and product relevance together. High impressions with zero clicks are a clue to investigate, not proof of a title problem.
- Content should fully serve the reader's task. The reason to continue into the product should be a real follow-up need, not a withheld answer.
- Check store or landing-page promises against the actual experience before conversion experiments. Verify platform mechanisms and field limits at execution time rather than treating them as permanent template facts.
- With limited traffic, documented manual observations or user conversations can help explain problems. Contact others only within the user's actual authorization.
- Existing payment or transaction signals can be investigated directly without validating every other funnel step first.
- Treat old audits and issue lists as historical findings until rechecked against the current release or page. Record verification date, scope, evidence, and status (Needs verification / Confirmed open / Resolved / Not applicable); only confirmed current issues become repair tasks. Preserve prior observations and do not silently update their dates.
- Correct verified factual errors, misleading promises, and experience failures promptly. Record their effect on experiments instead of waiting for new traffic data or the experiment's end.
